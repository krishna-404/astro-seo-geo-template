# Launch playbook — what happens when a new site goes live

Two launches, in order. The **technical launch** (SETUP Phases 0–4,
PLAYBOOK §8) makes the site correct and measurable; it is a gate, and
`marketing/ACTIONS.md § Launch` holds its checklist. The **announcement**
below makes the first inbound links, the first citations and the first
hour of signal exist at all. `/launch` runs both with the owner in the
session; this file is the plan it works.

Adapted from a tech-on-X launch playbook (Atomik, Sep 2026, see
`marketing/playbook-intake.md`) with its rented-network engine removed:
the choreography needs a network of 15 to 20 people the founder already
has, not 300 paid accounts. Every prompt that drafts launch copy from this
file ends with: Remove all mannered prose.

## 0. The gate — nothing is announced before this is true

- [ ] Every `launch` item in `marketing/ACTIONS.md` is ✅ (`npm run
      actions -- --phase launch`), the live smoke is green, and the owner
      has walked the live site on a phone.
- [ ] The landing page the announcement points at exists and is focused: a
      video pointing at a homepage with no explainer bleeds most of its
      visitors. The hero states the benefit in the first line; one CTA;
      the proof under it.
- [ ] `/about` is built to `page-guidelines.md § 3` and every key fact is
      approved in writing.
- [ ] Analytics records a pageview and the CTA events fire; Search Console
      and Bing are verified; the sitemap is submitted; IndexNow key is
      live. Otherwise the launch cannot be measured.
- [ ] The one KPI is chosen and written in `STRATEGY.md § 8`: enquiries
      through the form, demo bookings, sign-ups, pre-orders. Everything
      below points at that one number. Views are a vanity metric until a
      client confirms pipeline.

## 1. The timeline

The standard run is fourteen days; a compressed five-day version works
when the assets exist.

| Day | Move | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| T-14 | First working call: challenge the date, the hook, the copy | Come with a draft; expect all three to change |
| T-10 | Reframe the category so the buyer does not feel threatened | "AI CMO" became "autonomous AI marketer" because CMOs are the buyer. The category name goes into `STRATEGY.md § 1` and the About page's first sentence |
| T-7 | Pick the day off the calendar | Map the week's other launches and news; move off crowded slots. Tuesday 7–9am PT piles up; Monday early or mid-morning US Eastern is quieter. For India and the Gulf, LinkedIn first, X second, and the local morning |
| T-5 | Build the three tiers of amplifiers from people who exist | § 3 |
| T-3 | Write the custom copy per person; brief any creator to compare you head-on with the quarter's big launch | Each post in that person's own tone; two posts that read alike break the effect |
| T-3 to T-2 | Produce the asset | § 4. The founder's own Loom-style walkthrough beat polished video on impressions in at least one measured case; clarity and energy are the lever, not production value |
| T-1 | Calendar invite to investors, friends and early clients; 60 to 90 minute war-room call | Google Calendar or Luma; the invite carries the exact minute and the promo kit |
| T-0 | Founder posts; war room live through the day after | § 2 |
| T+2h to T+24h | Ride the debate; second wave from the founder's network | Visibility halves roughly every six hours; plan the second wave the same day |
| T+1 to T+7 | Daily: request indexing, read Search Console, reply to every comment, log every inbound link | § 5 |
| T+30 | Review against the one KPI; write the launch breakdown as a post with the real numbers | It is the first `first-hand-experience` piece the site can honestly carry |

## 2. The first hour

Platforms judge engagement velocity in the first 30 to 60 minutes and then
expand or kill the post. The order matters more than the volume: people
who know the founder go first, so the algorithm sees signal before any
paid or asked-for amplification arrives.

| Minute | Who | What |
|---|---|---|
| +00 | Founder | Posts the launch. Brand account reposts within a minute |
| +05 | Investors, advisors, the inner circle | Quote-post with why they backed it, in their own words (the promo kit) |
| +15 | Founder's friends, early clients | Substantive comments, not "congrats": a question, a use, a number |
| +30 | Tier 1 voices | Independent quote-posts and threads |
| +60 | Tier 2 and 3 | Reach-layer quotes, reposts, likes |
| +60 to +180 | Everyone else | Twenty to thirty posts inside a two- to three-hour window read as a moment |

Rules for the window:

- Post a couple of hours **after** any press story goes live, so the
  coverage sits under the post.
- Reply from the brand and the founder accounts in real time. Casual, never
  PR speak. The top comments are the first thing every new viewer reads.
- Staff a shared chat (WhatsApp or Slack) through the day after. When the
  platform goes down for forty minutes, the war room is why the launch
  survives.
- Keyword CTA: ask viewers to comment a word for early access or the
  guide. It converts and it feeds the comment count.
- Promote the winner while comments are still active, if promotion is
  budgeted; late boosts waste money because of decay.

## 3. Amplification without a rented network

Five jobs; the founder's own network outranks paid influencers.

1. **Tiers, each with one job.** Tier 1: five to ten voices the buyer
   already follows, for independent quote-posts and threads. Tier 2: the
   ICP-adjacent reach layer, for quotes and substantive comments. Tier 3:
   the momentum layer (everyone who will like and repost), to hold velocity
   through the day.
2. **Vet the audience, not the follower count.** Start from the buyer,
   look at who the buyers follow, cross-match with the network. A third of
   an amplifier's audience should be in the target market; 50K relevant
   followers beat 500K in the wrong geography.
3. **Custom copy per person**, drafted in their tone, approved by them.
   Two posts that read alike break the illusion.
4. **The promo kit for the inner circle.** Every founder has 15 to 20
   people who will repost if the friction is zero. Write each post from
   that person's point of view (the investor on why they backed it, the
   peer endorsing, the customer on actual use), send it with the calendar
   invite, copy-paste-post.
5. **Receipts over endorsements.** Brief early clients on the product;
   those who believe in it buy and post the order confirmation or the
   result. A real receipt gives followers permission to spend.

**Disclosure.** Paid amplification carries the platform's paid label.
Pre-written "organic" replies seeded under the post, and investor posts
written by an agency without the investor's own edit, are astroturf: this
site does not do them. Everything a person posts on the site's behalf, that
person has read, edited and chosen to post.

## 4. The asset and the hook

The hook is a provocation or a proof, not a description.

- **Lead with proof when trust is the barrier.** The raise, the named
  users, the backer, in the first line. No "revolutionary", no
  "game-changing" (`voice.json` bans them).
- **Pick a fight the site can win.** A named enemy (the dying model, the
  wrong strategy, the channel that stopped working, content-guidelines
  § 3) keeps a post alive past the decay curve. Never a person.
- **Tier the angle by audience.** Different first lines for different
  buyer groups.
- **Show more of yourself than feels comfortable.** Personality and
  honesty beat polish.
- **Keep the post copy minimal when the asset carries the message.**

The asset, if video: stop the scroll in three seconds; no logo open, no
"Hi, I'm the founder"; first frame shows the benefit; one job, one CTA;
60–90 s hero plus 15 s and 6 s cutdowns, three to five variants; 190–225
words for 90 s; burned-in captions, UI in the middle half of the frame;
9:16 for mobile discovery, 16:9 only for code or desktop demos; open on a
face; native upload. Frameworks that say the same thing: Hook, Problem,
Solution, Proof, CTA; or Hook (3–5 s), Reveal, Depth (one capability),
Context plus CTA. If no video exists, a founder screen recording with a
clear voice is the asset. The landing page embeds the same hero.

## 5. The first thirty days

Launch day is a spike; the thirty days after decide whether anything
compounds.

- **Daily (the cadence's daily run does the machine half):** request
  indexing for every new URL (ACTIONS A-D01); read the quick-win and BOFU
  blocks in `npm run insights`; reply to every comment and mention; log
  every inbound link and listing into `marketing/link-targets.md` as
  `live`.
- **Week 1:** the Tier 1 and Tier 2 directory listings claimed
  (`link-targets.md`); the About page's Key Facts reconfirmed after the
  launch changed anything (pricing, clients); the first prompt-panel run
  (`marketing/ai-panel.md`) as the baseline for who the assistants name.
- **Week 2:** the launch breakdown drafted from the real numbers (views,
  saves, replies, the KPI), published as the first case-study post, linked
  from the About page.
- **Week 3–4:** the second wave: clip the long-form (a recorded founder
  conversation, a demo) into short pieces for the channels the ICP uses;
  each carries the same enemy and the same message. Commentary and
  reaction formats count as amplification where the platform supports
  them.
- **Day 30 review:** the one KPI against `STRATEGY.md § 8`; what worked,
  what did not, into `marketing/field-notes.md`; `ACTIONS A-L14` dated.

## 6. Where this breaks

- A consumer product that needs mass reach: depth does not solve a volume
  problem.
- An offer that does not close yet: attention exposes the weakness faster.
- A founder who wants to be known, not booked: a different goal with a
  different playbook.
- A team that needs results inside 30 days: trust compounds and the first
  90 days feel slow by design.
- A category where the ICP is not on the launch platform (India and the
  Gulf: LinkedIn and Instagram carry founder and B2B attention; X is thin).
  Run the same choreography where they are.
